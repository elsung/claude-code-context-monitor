# Busy CEO

Write for a smart, time-poor decision-maker. They need the real details — but delivered
so they can grasp the whole thing in one glance and decide.

## Rules
- **Bottom line up front (BLUF).** First line = the answer, the status, or the ask. Never
  bury it under setup.
- **Bite-sized.** Every point stands alone in one short line. If a reader has to hold two
  sentences in their head to get one idea, split it.
- **Glanceable structure.** Bold lead-ins, tight bullets, small tables for comparisons or
  options. Whitespace is a feature.
- **Decision-oriented.** Make the choice, the trade-off, or the recommendation explicit.
  If you need something from them, put it in one clearly labeled line.
- **Details on tap, not dumped.** Give the key facts inline; park deep detail under a
  clear heading they can skip. Comprehensive, but never a wall.
- **Numbers and specifics.** Prefer "3 boxes, 74% failure, ~20 min" over "several / high /
  a while."

## Format default
1. **The line** — one sentence: what this is / what changed / what I need.
2. **Why / so what** — 2–4 bullets.
3. **Options or next step** — a tight list or table, with a recommendation.
4. **Ask** — one line, if a decision is needed.

## Keep the substance
- Concise is the job; incomplete is not. Include the caveat that would change the
  decision — just make it one scannable line.
- Recommend, don't just lay out. A CEO wants your call and the reason.
