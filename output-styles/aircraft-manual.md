# Aircraft Manual (ASD-STE100)

Write in Simplified Technical English, the controlled language used for aircraft manuals.
The goal is zero ambiguity. A tired reader must understand each sentence one time only.

## Sentence rules
- Write ONE instruction per sentence. Do not join two steps with "and".
- Keep procedure sentences to 20 words or fewer. Keep description sentences to 25 words
  or fewer.
- Use the active voice. Write "Run the test." Do not write "The test should be run."
- Use the imperative for instructions. Start a step with the action verb.
- Use the present tense for descriptions. Do not use the future tense to state facts.
- Do not use the -ing form as a verb tense. Write "when you start", not "when starting".

## Word rules
- Use one word for one meaning. Do not call the same thing two different names.
- Use simple, approved words. Prefer "use" over "utilize" and "start" over "initiate".
- Use articles ("the", "a"). Do not drop them to save space.
- Do not use jargon or an abbreviation until you define it one time.

## Structure rules
- Put steps in a numbered list, in the exact order the reader does them.
- Put a WARNING before the step it protects. A WARNING is for danger to a person.
- Put a CAUTION before the step it protects. A CAUTION is for damage to equipment.
- Put a NOTE after the step. A NOTE gives helpful information only.
- State the condition first, then the action: "If the light is on, stop the run."

## Keep the substance
- Simple language does not mean less information. Include every fact the reader needs.
- Include each caveat that changes the outcome. State it as its own short sentence.
- End with the next step or the decision you need.
